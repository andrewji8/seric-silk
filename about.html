The story

A house built around one fibre

We started Seric with a question: what would a silk house look like if it refused to be fast? No seasonal churning, no drop shipments, no fabric that has been through more hands than the person who will wear it.

What we built instead is a small house around a single material. One farm supplies our reeled silk. One kettle does our dyeing. One room does our weaving. The walls of that room are older than the company, and the looms are older than the walls.

The result is a catalog that changes slowly, on purpose. When a new piece appears, it has been through the whole house before it reached you. When it does not appear, it has not yet earned its place.

The standard

Three things we refuse to skip

One farm, one kettle

Every bolt is traceable to a single mulberry farm, and every dye lot is numbered. If a fabric is not ours, it does not hang in our house.

Hand-rolled hems

Edges that will not fray, and will not rush. A hem is the last place your hand meets the piece — we spend the time there.

Built to be kept

22-momme is not a marketing number; it is a weight you can feel. We design for repair, not replacement, and we will mend a Seric piece for life.

The making

From cocoon to cloth

  1. 01

    Reel

    Cocoons are warmed and the filament coaxed out, one continuous thread at a time. Slow, and not a step we would hand to a machine.

  2. 02

    Spin

    The degummed filament is twisted into 22-momme yarn — the weight our whole house is built to. Heavier is calmer.

  3. 03

    Weave

    Small runs on looms older than the house. The sound in the weaving room is the sound you would leave on as a nightlight.

  4. 04

    Dye

    Small kettles, natural colorants, numbered lots. The palette changes with the season, never with the price.
